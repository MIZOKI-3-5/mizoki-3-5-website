Trust & governance

Trust comes from governance, not automation.

This page does not begin with artificial intelligence, because trust doesn't. It begins with who holds authority, what the rules are, how every decision is recorded, and how anything can be undone. AI contributes inside those rails — never around them.

Honesty note: capabilities below are labeled Live when they run in production on the MIZ OKI platform today, Pilot-ready when they are exercised and validated during a pilot, and Roadmap when they are direction, not present functionality. We make no compliance-certification claims anywhere on this site.

The governance model

Ten commitments, plainly stated

Human authority Live

Authority is explicit configuration: named approvers, delegated rules, bounded automation grants. Nothing acts outside a grant, and the default everywhere is observe-only.

Policy enforcement Live

Financial floors, budget ceilings, brand and risk rules are checked mechanically on every candidate action. A veto is recorded with the exact check that failed — never silently.

Explainability Live

Every recommendation carries its reasoning: the evidence, the hypotheses tested, the alternatives compared, the checks applied. If it cannot explain itself, it does not ship as a recommendation.

Audit trail Live

Decisions are append-only records: evidence, context, reasoning, policy results, authority, action, outcome — inspectable after the fact, attributable at every step.

Rollback capability Pilot-ready

The rollback path is recorded before an action dispatches, not improvised after it misfires. Humans hold rollback authority at all times.

Evidence provenance Live

Every observation carries source, timestamp, quality, and identity resolution. Evidence that cannot be traced does not carry decisions.

Decision replay Live

Any decision can be re-walked with the evidence as it stood at the time. Deterministic engines mean the same inputs reproduce the same trace — replay is how our own demos are verified.

Confidence scoring Pilot-ready

Confidence is measured against realized outcomes, decision after decision — a track record, not a self-report. Calibration is visible, including when it worsens.

Security model Pilot-ready

Principles: least-privilege source access, role-based authorization on every surface, admin functions gated behind authenticated sessions, secrets held in managed secret stores — reviewed together during pilot scoping against your requirements.

Privacy considerations Pilot-ready

Consent-first evidence boundaries are set explicitly at scoping: what may be observed, at what granularity, with what identity resolution. The system decides with commercial signals — it is not in the surveillance business.

Executive proof

How your hardest evaluators would test this

Six desks, six different questions. The answers are the same governance model, seen from six angles.

The CFO asks

"What stops this from spending money badly, faster?" — Financial floors and ceilings are enforced before any recommendation exists; every action above a grant needs a named approver; every prediction is scored against the realized outcome, so the system's judgment has an inspectable track record.

Legal asks

"Who is accountable when it acts?" — A person or an explicit grant, always, recorded per decision. The append-only record shows who approved what, on what evidence, under which policy — the opposite of an unexplainable black box.

Security asks

"What can it reach, and how is that controlled?" — Scoped, least-privilege connections defined at pilot scoping; role-based authorization; no action pathways outside the granted surface. The security review is part of scoping, not an afterthought.

Marketing asks

"Will this replace our judgment?" — No. It replaces the archaeology before the judgment: evidence assembled, causes tested, options compared. The calls that matter route to your team, with better material.

Operations asks

"What happens when it's wrong?" — The rollback path was recorded before dispatch; the wrong prediction is scored publicly in outcome memory; the decision class loses confidence weight. Wrongness is metabolized, not hidden.

Procurement asks

"What are we actually buying?" — A scoped 90-day pilot with agreed metrics, explicit exit criteria, and no default renewal. The deliverable is evidence about your own decisions — you keep it under any exit.

Bright lines

What we will not claim

No certification theater

We name our security and privacy principles and review them against your requirements. We do not claim certifications we have not earned.

No outcome promises

Illustrative numbers are labeled illustrative, everywhere, and never presented as verified production performance. The pilot measures; it does not presume.

No quiet autonomy

Authority never expands on its own. Every increase is a human decision, made on the record, reversible like everything else.

If a claim on this site is not labeled, it is a commitment. If it is labeled, the label is the claim.
