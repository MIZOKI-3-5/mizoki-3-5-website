The way in

The 90-Day Causal Growth Control Pilot

Begin observe-only. Advance to human-approved actions only after evidence quality, economics, and policy boundaries are validated. Autonomy is earned, never assumed — every advance in authority is a deliberate, human-made decision backed by the pilot's own evidence. Pilot-ready

Deliberately narrow

Pilot scope

A pilot that tries to govern everything proves nothing. This one scopes tightly so its evidence is unambiguous.

  • One brand or business unit
  • Limited media and commerce sources
  • One performance-incident decision class
  • One budget-allocation or profit decision class
  • Explicit evidence boundaries
  • Explicit human authority
  • Observe-only starting mode
  • Measured predicted-versus-realized outcomes
  • Clear expansion criteria

Three phases

The structured plan

1

Days 1–30 · Connect and observe

The system earns the right to an opinion before it offers one.

  • Connect the scoped systems and confirm source access
  • Validate identities, timestamps, and economics
  • Establish canonical event definitions
  • Map the critical entities and constraints
  • Run in observe-only mode — no recommendations, no actions

Governance checkpoint: evidence-quality review with your team. The pilot does not advance until the evidence base is trusted.

2

Days 31–60 · Diagnose and recommend

Recommendations appear — every one of them for human review, none of them acting.

  • Generate causal recommendations for the scoped decision classes
  • Evaluate counterfactuals, including the no-action baseline
  • Measure and report confidence on every recommendation
  • Validate every candidate against configured policy
  • Require human approval on everything — no exceptions in this phase

Governance checkpoint: recommendation-quality review. Your team scores the diagnoses against ground truth before any action authority is discussed.

3

Days 61–90 · Approve, act, and learn

Narrowly scoped actions — human-approved, bounded, reversible — and honest scoring of the results.

  • Enable limited governed actions within explicit bounds
  • Preserve rollback paths on every action
  • Measure predicted versus realized outcomes
  • Produce the executive scorecard
  • Recommend an expansion roadmap — or recommend stopping

Governance checkpoint: exit review against the criteria below. Expansion is a decision you make on the pilot's record, not a renewal that happens by default.

How the pilot is judged

Success metrics and exit criteria

Success metrics

Agreed before day one, measured throughout, reported without editing:

  • evidence completeness and quality
  • time-to-detection on scoped incidents
  • diagnostic accuracy vs. ground truth
  • recommendation acceptance rate
  • policy-check coverage and veto record
  • prediction accuracy on acted decisions
  • decision latency, sense to authority

Targets for each metric are set with your team during scoping — the pilot commits to measuring honestly, not to hitting a number we invented before seeing your data.

Exit criteria

The pilot ends in one of three explicit states:

Expand
The scorecard clears the agreed thresholds — the expansion roadmap names the next decision classes, sources, and authority grants.
Extend
Evidence is promising but incomplete — a defined extension with named gaps and a firm end date.
Stop
The record does not justify continuing — you keep the scorecard, the decision records, and everything the pilot learned about your stack.

The pilot's product is evidence about your own decisions — whichever exit you choose, you keep it.
