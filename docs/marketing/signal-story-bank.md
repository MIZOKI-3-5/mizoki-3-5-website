# Signal Intelligence — Customer Story Bank v1.1
*Composite field notes for sales, briefing deck, site, and content. All numbers illustrative until replaced by verified pilots. v1.1 adds Stories 7–8 (net yield, cold start) per the Shopify/Net Contribution positioning.*

## The rules (non-negotiable)
1. **Label honestly.** Every story is a "composite scenario, illustrative numbers" until a real customer signs off on a named case study. Never invent company names, logos, or quotes attributed to people.
2. **One grammar, every story:** *their question → the quiet test → what the ledger showed → what changed.* Repetition teaches the product without a glossary.
3. **Translate, don't define.** Holdout = "we quietly kept the ads away from a small identical slice." Ghost bids = "we noted the exact moments an ad *would* have shown — without showing it." Incrementality = "what your money actually caused." Net contribution = "what an order actually nets after everything it costs you." Confidence interval = "how sure we are, printed on the slide."
4. **Preview capabilities stay preview.** Intent prediction (Story 6), net-yield bidding (Story 7), and cold-start seeding (Story 8) are told with in-development framing until their cells ship. Research-backed approaches cite the research; published results from other companies (e.g., Airbnb's synthetic-data metrics) are NEVER presented as ours.
5. **Never a guaranteed outcome.** Stories end in decisions and receipts, not promised percentages for the listener.

## Story 1 — The Doorman (retargeting)
**Persona:** DTC / e-commerce growth lead. **Pain:** dashboard says retargeting is the hero; growth is flat.
**30-second talk track:** "Your dashboard says retargeting returns 8x. Here's the uncomfortable question it can't answer: how many of those people were coming back anyway? One team let us quietly hold the ads back from a small, identical slice of shoppers. That slice bought almost as often as everyone else. The real return was about 1.4x. They cut the channel by a third, moved the money to net-new customers, and revenue held. You were paying a doorman to open a door people were already walking through."
**The metaphor to reuse:** *the doorman.*
**Objection it preempts:** "our retargeting numbers are great."

## Story 2 — Paying For Your Own Name (brand search)
**Persona:** CMO fielding CFO pressure. **Pain:** the annual "why do we bid on our own brand?" fight.
**30-second talk track:** "Every company has this fight, and both sides are guessing. So stop guessing: turn brand ads off in a handful of cities, leave them on in their statistical twins, and read the registers six weeks later. In the composite we model, roughly four in five of those clicks just slid down to the free listing. The finding ended the fight — spend was rightsized, and the CFO got a confidence interval instead of an opinion."
**Metaphor:** *the free listing one inch below.*
**Objection:** "if we stop bidding, competitors steal the click." (Answer: the test measures exactly that, per market.)

## Story 3 — The Unneeded Coupon (lifecycle/email)
**Persona:** lifecycle/CRM marketer. **Pain:** discounts drive orders but margins shrink and unsubscribes climb.
**30-second talk track:** "The email report asks 'did they convert after the send?' The margin question is 'would they have converted without it?' Those are different customers. Signal sorts them: in the composite, about a fifth of discount sends went to sure-buyers — pure margin given away — and a smaller group was being actively pushed toward unsubscribe. Stop couponing the sure-buyers, go quiet on the annoyed, and margin recovers without one extra send."
**Metaphor:** *couponing things already in the cart.*
**Objection:** "email is our cheapest channel." (Cheap to send, expensive in margin.)

## Story 4 — Prove It Or Lose It (CTV/brand)
**Persona:** brand marketer defending unmeasurable spend. **Pain:** finance wants to cut what can't be tracked.
**30-second talk track:** "Streaming TV can't be measured click by click — so measure it the honest way: run it in some markets and deliberately not in their twins. Composite result: real lift in nine of fourteen markets, nothing defensible in five. Double down on nine, cut five. The budget came out smaller and unkillable, because every remaining dollar had a receipt."
**Metaphor:** *every dollar left had a receipt.*
**Objection:** "brand can't be measured." (It can — at the market level.)

## Story 5 — The Vendor Legal Blocked (governance as advantage)
**Persona:** marketing ops / RevOps at a regulated or privacy-sensitive company. **Pain:** every data tool dies in legal review.
**30-second talk track:** "Their last intent-data vendor died in legal review — nobody could explain where the data came from. Signal passed because the answer is short: consent is checked before anything is stored, sensitive categories are refused at the schema level, no audio exists anywhere in the system, and a customer's right to be forgotten cascades through every store. The privacy review that killed the last tool became the reason this one shipped."
**Metaphor:** *the review that killed the last tool approved this one.*
**Objection:** "legal will never approve another tracking tool."

## Story 6 — The Tuesday Morning List (anticipatory intent — PREVIEW, future tense only)
**Persona:** B2B demand gen / sales-aligned marketer. **Pain:** by the time a form is filled, the buying decision is mostly made.
**30-second talk track:** "Here's how the morning goes when the anticipatory capability ships: instead of waiting for a form fill, the team opens a short list — accounts whose behavior says they're entering the market now, each with a probability that means what it says and the reasoning path behind it. And one rule keeps it honest: acting on a predicted account always runs against a held-out comparison, so we can tell you whether the outreach *caused* the deal or just got there early. Prediction never gets to grade itself."
**Metaphor:** *the prediction never grades itself.*
**Required framing:** "in development / preview" — no present-tense deployment claims until ORACLE is live.

## Story 7 — The Bundle That Lost Money (net yield — PREVIEW)
**Persona:** Shopify founder / DTC operator. **Pain:** revenue up, cash down; the hero product hides its own costs.
**30-second talk track:** "Their $180 bundle was the best seller, and cash kept shrinking. Signal priced every order at what it truly nets — components, packing, dimensional shipping, payment fees, and the returns coming back. The ledger said the hero bundle netted somewhere between minus two and sixteen dollars an order once a third of them boomeranged. The boring single SKU netted thirty-one and almost never returned. So the bidding signal switched from checkout revenue to net contribution, and the ad platforms started hunting buyers who keep what they buy. Revenue flattened. Cash grew."
**Metaphor:** *the dashboard applauded the bundle; the bank account preferred the boring one.*
**Objection:** "our ROAS is fine." (ROAS can't see your returns, your packing line, or your shipping surcharges.)
**Required framing:** net-yield bidding is "in development / preview"; return-rate context (25–40% in apparel) cited as industry data, not customer result.

## Story 8 — Day One (cold start — PREVIEW)
**Persona:** new Shopify store / new SKU launch. **Pain:** every tool says "come back when you have data"; platform learning phases burn the launch budget.
**30-second talk track:** "New stores get told to pay for weeks of 'learning phase' — random exploration billed at full price. Instead, Mizoki seeds the targeting models with statistically realistic synthetic shoppers built from the catalog itself, an approach drawn from published enterprise research, then lets real signals take over as they arrive. The expensive exploration compresses, and the first ad dollar goes out with a hypothesis behind it instead of a shrug."
**Metaphor:** *new stores don't lack intelligence — they lack history, and history can be scaffolded.*
**Objection:** "AI tools need our data first."
**Required framing:** "in development / preview"; the approach follows published work (Airbnb synthetic search data, Amazon COSMO) — their published metrics are theirs, never quoted as ours.

## Upgrade path
When a real pilot completes: replace the illustrative number with the measured one, get written customer approval, relabel that story "pilot result," and move it to the top of every surface. One verified story outranks eight composites — and the honesty of the composite labeling is what makes the first verified one believable.
